Day 5 Phase B: Student Challenge 
— Document Ownership & Staleness by Team
 
**The Delta:** Today's demo answered one workload question about
`Ticket`s. Your challenge is the same pattern — build a DataFrame,
merge in `team`, group, compute with numpy — applied to `Document`s
instead: for each team, how many documents does it own, and what
share of those are stale?
 
### Requirements
 
1. Create `app\analytics\ownership.py`, following `workload.py`'s
   shape: module-level constants, a `_documents_to_frame` /
   `_users_to_frame` pair, and a `compute_document_ownership(documents,
   users)` function returning a plain dict.
2. A document counts as **stale** using the exact same rule
   `get_stale_documents()` already applies elsewhere in this codebase:
   `days_since_reviewed() > 90`, **and** its category is not
   `POSTMORTEM`. Don't invent a second staleness definition — reuse
   the existing one's logic.
3. For each team that owns at least one document, compute: how many
   documents it owns, how many of those are stale, and what
   percentage of its owned documents are stale (`0` if it owns none —
   don't divide by zero). Flag a team as a staleness risk if that
   percentage is `50` or higher.
4. Add `TeamDocumentOwnership` and `DocumentOwnershipReport` schemas to
   `api\schemas.py`, following `TeamWorkload`/`WorkloadReport`'s exact
   shape.
5. Add `get_document_ownership_report()` to `KnowledgeBaseService`, and
   a new `GET /analytics/document-ownership` route on the same
   `analytics` router from today's demo (not a new router — add the
   second route to the existing `api\routers\analytics.py`).
### Constraints
 
- Reuse the `analytics` router already created in Step 7 — don't
  create a second `APIRouter` with its own `/analytics` prefix.
- Guard the zero-denominator case (a team owning zero documents) the
  same careful way Step 3's `total_load > 0` check does for workload —
  don't let a team with no documents produce a `NaN` or a crash.
- Before you trust whatever your AI assistant suggests for the
  percentage/threshold logic, apply the same scrutiny Step 4
  practiced: feed it a team with zero owned documents and a team
  whose count is a value like `1` (where percentages round strangely)
  and confirm the suggestion handles both correctly.
### Research Prompts
 
- `pd.crosstab(...)` can produce a team-by-status count table directly
  from two Series, without an explicit `.groupby(...).size()` call.
  Look up its signature and consider whether it would simplify (or
  complicate) today's workload calculation if teams needed to be
  broken down by ticket status *and* priority at once.
- `numpy.percentile()` is a different, non-parametric way to flag
  outliers than "more than one standard deviation above the mean" —
  useful when a distribution isn't roughly bell-shaped. With only two
  or three teams in this dataset the difference won't show up
  clearly, but it's worth knowing the mean+std approach isn't the only
  legitimate choice.
- pandas' `.agg(...)` can compute multiple aggregations (like `count`
  and `sum`) from a single `.groupby(...)` call, instead of calling
  `.groupby(...)` twice the way `compute_team_workload` currently
  does for `counts` and `load`. Look up its syntax and consider
  whether combining them would make the function clearer or just more
  compact.
